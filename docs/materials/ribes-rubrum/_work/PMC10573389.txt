Introduction

Cultivatedspecies, commonly referred to as “currant” and “gooseberry”, are popular and old small berry crops in Europe and Asia (;). Blackcurrant (), red currant (), white currant (), and gooseberry () () are dominant cultivated taxa due to their high yield, fruit quality, and ecological adaptability (). In 2021, the global cultivation area of currants and gooseberries was 148,275 hectares, with a fruit production of 0.82 million tons, reaching a gross value of 1,503 million USD (FAOSTAT,). Currants and gooseberries are commonly processed into juice, wine, candy, and jam owing to their high concentrations of functional metabolites and distinctive flavors (). In recent years, they have been reported to contain multi-functional bioactive metabolites, such as anthocyanins, polyphenols, phenolic acids, and flavonoids, and are characterized by potential antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial bioactivities that are involved in antidiabetic, antineoplastic, anti-tumor, anti-cardiovascular, and anti-neurodegenerative functions (;). Despite their great economic importance and health potential, there has been little research on thespecies to date, which not only lags far behind that on major fresh fruits such as apple, citrus, and grape, but also falls behind that on emerging fruit crops that are mainly supplied for the processing market, such as sea-buckthorn (;), blue honeysuckle berry (), and wolfberry (). In particular, the phylogeny of Grossulariaceae and the infrageneric relationships of thegenus remain highly controversial ().

Based on morphology,was once included in the polyphyletic family Saxifragaceae () but is now more accepted as the sole genus in the Grossulariaceae family (). However, because some literature still places Grossulariaceae under Saxifragaceae, the delineation of the Grossulariaceae family remains blurry (), and its monophyletic origin has been rarely discussed in previous studies because only a limited number ofspecies were used (). In addition, the infrageneric classification ofremains unclear due to its wide geographical distribution, unknown interspecific hybridization, and high phenotypic diversity (;). From the first monograph of Janczewski on(), the infrageneric classification ofhas been constantly proposed and revised (;;;;;), resulting in a highly confusing taxonomy. In the work of Janczewski, thegenus was divided into six subgenera with eleven sections (,(and),(and),(,,,and),, and(and)) (). Moreover, Berger divided Grossulariaceae into thegenus (currants, including eight subgenera) and thegenus (gooseberries, including four subgenera). This classification was later demoted to two corresponding subgenera with 12 sections, which is the most consensus and widely accepted classification ofso far (;;;;). Namely, thesubgenus includes eight sections:(alpine currant),(red-flowering currant),(blackcurrant),(spiny currant),(skunk currant),(Andine currant),(red currant),(golden currant), and thesubgenus includes four sections:(true gooseberry),(Sierra gooseberry),(ornamental gooseberry), and(desert gooseberry). Although internal transcribed spacer, nontranscribed spacer, and chloroplast markers have been recently applied to resolve the infrageneric phylogeny of(;;), inconsistent results have been reported regarding the monophyly of gooseberries (subgenus) and the phylogenetic relationship among the above-mentioned 12 sections. Additionally, the genomic data ofhave been exclusively used in studies of the evolution of Saxifragales (;) and the genetic diversity of endemicpopulations ().

Plastids are semi-autonomous organelles in the plant cell, carrying out photosynthesis, and various metabolic and signaling functions (;). Due to the haploidy, uniparental inheritance, no genetic recombination, conserved genomic structure, and relatively small genome size of plastids, the polymorphic loci of plastids have been increasingly applied to plant phylogenetics, taxonomy, population genetics, and marker development for molecular breeding and DNA barcoding (;;;). Meanwhile, the possibility that a lack of variation in the modest number of plastid genes may hamper species discrimination, suggesting improved resolutions based on complete plastome sequences (;). Through comparative analysis based on the complete chloroplast (cp) sequences, the phylogeny of some plants, such as,, Orchidaceae, and Myrtales, has been delineated from the species level to the order level (;;;). However, only fivecp genomes have been released in NCBI to date (Jan. 2023). Among them,andvar.() are wild species indigenous to East Asia; and Sierra currant (), Sierra gooseberry () (), and clove currant () () are endemic species of America. There has been no report about the cp genomes of cultivatedspecies and their phylogenies, which has severely limited the germplasm exploration, genetic conservation, cross-breeding, and genetic improvement of.

Herein, complete cp genomes of blackcurrant (), red currant and white currant (), and gooseberry () were assembled and characterized, laying a molecular foundation for the research ontaxa. The study also identified the highly polymorphic sites across all the nine releasedcp genomes, providing practical tools for molecular breeding and DNA barcoding. Moreover, phylogenetic trees of Saxifragales were constructed by using 31 complete cp genomes, which can shed new light on the phylogeny of the Grossulariaceae family andspecies.

Materials and Methods

Plant materials and DNA extraction

The fresh leaves of blackcurrant ‘Liangye houpi’ (), red currant ‘Red Cross’ (), white currant ‘Witte Parel’ (), and gooseberry ‘Pixwell’ () were collected at the Horticultural Station of Northeast Agricultural University (126.73°E, 45.74°N), Harbin, China. Samples were frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen and kept at an ultra-low temperature freezer (–80 °C) until DNA extraction. Total genomic DNA was isolated using the Hi-Fast Plant Genomic DNA Kit (GeneBetter BioTech Co. Ltd., Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions ().

Sequencing, assembly, and annotation

The DNA library preparation and next-generation sequencing were accomplished atCo., Ltd. (Wuhan, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions (MGI Tech Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) of the DNBSEQ-T7 platform. The adaptors of the raw reads were removed using Trimmomatic v0.93 () and then the reads were filtered using SOAPnuke v2.X () with parameters of “–lowQual=20, –nRate=0.005, –qualRate=0.5”. The four cp genomes were assembled using GetOrganelle v1.7.6.1 () with parameters of “-R 15 -k 21,45,65,85,105” and with the reference ofvar.(). Genomic annotation was performed using CPGAVAS2 () and further examined manually according to() and() (). The cp feature map was constructed using OGDRAW v1.3.1 () with default parameters.

Comparative genomic analysis

The five previously reportedcp genomes were re-annotated using CPGAVS2 with the reference ofvar.(). The ninecp genomes were aligned using MAFFT v7.471 (). The pairwise genetic divergence was calculated using BioEdit v7.2.6 (). The IR boundary comparison of the ninewas performed using IRSCOPE (). mVISTA program () was used to visualize the alignments of the ninecp genomes with the reference ofvar..

Repeat, SNP, and indel identification

Tandem Repeats Finder () was used to determine tandem repeats with default parameters. REPuter () was used to determine forward (F), palindromic (P), reverse (R), and complement (C) repeats with the parameters of “Hamming Distance: 3; Maximum Computed Repeats: 5,000 bp; Minimum Repeat Size: 30 bp”. SSR loci were identified using Misa-web () () with default parameters. SNPs and Indels among the nine cp genomes were identified using DnaSP v6.12 () and thevalues were calculated with a window length of 600 bp and a step size of 200 bp.

Phylogenetic analysis

The complete cp genomes of 20 representative species in Saxifragales, four focal taxa in the present study, five previously releasedspecies, and two outgroup species (Rosales) were selected for phylogenetic analysis and their Genbank accession numbers are shown in. The multi-sequence alignment was formalized using trimAL (). The maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA v11.0.13 in the multithread mode with 1,000 bootstrap (BS) replications () and further annotated using iTOL v6 online () (). The Bayesian tree was constructed using BEAST 2.7.5 () following the standard workflow () and referenced the literature reported by. The confidences of the clades were evaluated by using posterior probabilities (PP).

Identification of repeat elements among the fourcp genomes

Aiming to provide useful information for marker development, the repeat elements in the four cp genomes were annotated. A total of 219 long repeats were identified (), including tandem, forward, and palindromic repeats but not reverse and complement repeats. Tandem repeats accounted for the largest proportion (44.7%), followed by palindromic repeats (30.1%) and then forward repeats (25.1%) (and). For blackcurrant (), red/white currant (), and gooseberry (), 53, 51, and 64 long repeats were identified, respectively. A total of 176 SSR loci were identified in the four cp genomes, including mononucleotides (97.2%) and dinucleotides (2.8%). In addition, 54, 40, and 42 SSRs were identified in blackcurrant (), red/white currant (), and gooseberry (), respectively (and). SSRs showed the highest distribution frequency in the LSC (32–40), followed by the SSC (4–7), and then the IRs (2–4) (). Moreover, SSRs had the highest distribution frequency in the intergenic region (28–33), followed by the intron region (6–9), and the CDS (3–17) ().

Phylogenomic analysis of Grossulariaceae

To determine the phylogenetic position of Grossulariaceae in Saxifragales, ML and Bayesian phylogenetic trees were constructed based on 31 complete cp genomes, including the above-mentioned nine, 20 representative species from Saxifragales and two outgroup species from Rosales (). The ML and Bayesian tree topology consensually revealed seven well-supported main clades in Saxifragales (BS ≥ 93% and PP = 100%), including clade I, Grossulariaceae; clade II, Saxifragaceae; clade III, Iteaceae; clade IV, Cercidiphyllaceae and Altingiaceae; clade V, Paeoniaceae; clade VI, Crassulaceae, Haloragaceae, and Penthoraceae; and clade VII, Hamamelidaceae (and). Notably, the ninetaxa were clustered independently to form clade I with maximum BS and PP values instead of nesting within Saxifragaceae (clade II), indicating the monophyly of Grossulariaceae. Discussion

The present study for the first time assembled and characterized the complete cp genomes of four cultivatedtaxa, including blackcurrant (), red currant and white currant (), and gooseberry (), which enriches the genomic foundation for. The Grossulariaceae showed relatively high chloroplast conservativeness in terms of sequence length, structural organization, gene content, order, and arrangement than other plant families such as Gentianaceae, Asteraceae, Caprifoliaceae, and the genetic close family, Saxifragaceae (;;;). Intriguingly, the results showed that the IR/SC shift types of the ninecp genomes are in accordance with the phylogenetic groups (,and), suggesting the shrinkage and expansion of the IR/SC boundaries might serve as an additional confirmation for phylogenetic results that calculated from the algorithms based on sequences. Notably, the cp genomes of red currant () and white currant () are 100% identical, which supports the hypothesis: white currant is an albino cultivar of red currant (;;) and calls for further verification based on nuclear data. Repeat elements play an important role in sequence divergence and are involved in plastome rearrangement (;). Herein, 51/40, 64/42, and 53/54 long-repeats/SSR-loci was respectively identified in,, andcp genomes, whereas that of the wildis 49/56 (). These differences not merely display the characteristic evolutionary trace of these plastomes but also provide effective information for species discrimination and population genetics. Moreover, a total of 3,322 SNPs and 485 Indels were identified in the nine releasedcp genomes. In particular, the intergeneric spacer of, the CDS of, and the introns ofandshowed high-resolution potentials. The molecular markers developed from these loci may contribute to genetic evaluations for germplasm collections, conservation genetic research for endangered species or enigmatic taxa, DNA barcoding for endemic species or important cultivars, and hybrid identification in cross-breeding ofin the future.

In terms of the phylogeny of Grossulariaceae, we for the first time used the genomic data ofspecies to clarify the monophyly of Grossulariaceae and the paraphyletic relationship between Grossulariaceae and Saxifragaceae, which are consistent with the results of previous studies about the phylogeny of Saxifragales (,). Moreover, in both ML and Bayesian trees, the highly supported co-ancestral clade of Saxifragaceae and Grossulariaceae and the relatively later divergence between them were revealed, suggesting their close phylogenetic relationship, which is in accordance with the previous studies based on various data types (;;). In terms of the infrageneric relationships, our results generally support the consensus classification ofproposed byand revised by, that is, thegenus includes 12 sections of,,,,,,,,,,and, which is also supported by recent studies based on molecular markers (;;;). Although our results revealed a well-supported clade of the subgenus, only two species (and) in this subgenus were used. Therefore, the monophyletic origin ofcannot be asserted here. However, in any case, the clade of thesubgenus is nested within thesubgenus and shows a paraphyletic relationship with the co-ancestor of theandsections either in the present study or in previous studies (;), which is incongruent to that of the generally accepted subclassification based on morphological characteristics of thegenus, regardless of whether gooseberry () is monophyletic. However, according to the limited samples and only cp genomic data used in the present study, the thorough clarification of intrageneric phylogeny and infrageneric resolution, evolutional scenario, and diversification history ofstill call for comprehensive studies based on large-scale sample collection, nuclear genomic data, biogeographic information, and fossil evidence.

Conclusions

The complete cp genomes of blackcurrant (), red currant and white currant (), and gooseberry () have lengths of 157,450–157,802 bp and share a total of 131 genes (86 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes). Notably, a 100% sequence identity was observed between red currant and white currant, supporting the inference that the latter is an albino cultivar of the former. A total of 485 Indels and 3,322 SNPs were identified in the nine releasedcp genomes.(= 0.020) and(= 0.021) are the most polymorphic genic and intergenic regions, respectively. The ML and Bayesian phylogenetic trees provide strong support for the monophyly of Grossulariaceae in Saxifragales and the paraphyletic relationship between Saxifragaceae and Grossulariaceae. The tree topology of the ninetaxa shows that thesubgenus is nested within thesubgenus, and has a paraphyletic relationship with the co-ancestor of theandsections. Our findings lay a foundation for the phylogenetic research and molecular breeding ofspecies.
